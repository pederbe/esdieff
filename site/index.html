Explore interfaces, catch mistakes, spot breaking changes and turn model constraints into useful tests.

Esdieff is a command-line toolkit and Rust library for connected-device models written in Semantic Definition Format, or SDF. Use it with models you create, receive from a partner or find in a public collection.

See it in action

One model rule.
Four test values.

A device model describes readings, settings, commands and events. Esdieff works with any SDF model; the supplied thermostat is just the example that ships with it. Here is one of its rules, and what Esdieff makes of it.

This thermostat model accepts a target temperature from 5 to 30 °C in half-degree steps. The rule, as written in thermostat.sdf.json:

thermostat.sdf.json JSON 
1"targetTemperature": {2  "description": "Requested room temperature in half-degree increments",3  "type": "number",4  "nullable": false,5  "minimum": 5,6  "maximum": 30,7  "multipleOf": 0.5,8  "unit": "Cel",9  "readable": true,10  "writable": true11}

Generate values from its rules with Esdieff:

Terminal 
$ esdieff generate --document thermostat.sdf.json thermostat.sdf.jsonGenerated values esdieff.generation/1: passedValues: 15 admitted, 60 rejected, 0 undeterminedDescriptions: 4

Here are four of the generated target-temperature values:

Generated valueResultWhy
5 °CAcceptedAt the lower boundary
30 °CAcceptedAt the upper boundary
31 °CRejectedAbove the maximum
5.25 °CRejectedOutside the half-degree steps

Bring values like these into your firmware or application tests.
